# Stretching the project without losing depth

A stretch earns nothing if the brief underneath it is thin. The rule: a stretch starts only after the cold run passes on the base claim, and it must deepen the same question rather than open a new one. Each idea below says what it adds and what it must not replace.

## Stretches that deepen the brief

| Brief | Stretch | What it adds | Must not replace |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · Growth | Turn the tree into a what-if: if the short branch recovered to plan, how many more tests, on what assumption? | A number the board can act on, and an explicit assumption. | The branch split with both years numbered. |
| A · Growth | Rebuild the tree in revenue as well as volume, using results and invoices, and compare the two trees. | Shows whether the volume gap is also a money gap. | The volume tree. |
| B · Two cities | Write the rule Kalpa Health should follow the next time a city changes systems, with the three checks that would have caught this fall in week one. | A level-five answer. | The counting-versus-real split. |
| B · Two cities | Reconstruct one week of bookings from both systems by hand and show the row-level mapping. | Evidence the finance controller can touch. | The city-by-quarter table. |
| C · Money | Turn the bridge into a weekly check: which two numbers to compare, what threshold triggers an alert, where the alert goes. | A control, not just a finding. | The bridge with both sides reconciled. |
| C · Money | Age the unpaid invoices: how many days since invoice, in buckets, by channel. | Separates late money from lost money. | The unmatched lists. |

## Stretches that apply to every brief

- **The sensitivity table.** For the three decisions that moved the headline most, show the headline under the alternative decision. Three rows. The panel will love it and it takes an hour if the decisions log is complete.
- **The one-page memo.** Four paragraphs in Dr Menon's voice to her board: claim, evidence, caveat, action. Write it after the slides. It tests whether the claim survives without a diagram.
- **The second group's answer.** If another group holds the same brief, read their repository after the freeze and write one paragraph on where you differ and why. Do not change your own work.
- **The static web view.** Export the headline chart and the one sentence to a single HTML page in `docs/` that opens without a login. Plain HTML from the notebook is enough. It earns no extra marks; it is a reasonable thing to have in a portfolio.

## Stretches that are not stretches

- Adding a dashboard before the claim is defended.
- Trying a model. Week 5 teaches models. A regression on three days of cleaning is a wall you will have to explain.
- Using a library nobody in the group can explain line by line.
- Bringing in outside data. The caveat is the place to say what you wish you had.
- Prettifying slides past six. The seventh slide is where time goes to die.

## How to tell whether a stretch is worth it

Ask three questions. If any answer is no, do not start it.

1. Does the base claim already pass the cold run and the hostile-reader test?
2. Does the stretch deepen the same question, so that a panel question about it leads back to the claim?
3. Can one member finish it in under three hours without pulling anyone else off the base?
